Foundational Frame · Duties of Office and Their Trespass

The Ethical Breach is Paramount.
The Law is its Instrument.

Cicero's De Officiis (44 BC) is a treatise on duties: on what attaches to a station, as distinct from what the law happens to require of it. Written to a son entering public life, it fixes the order of the argument this Tairiscint follows. Justice has two offices and one foundation. Its first office is that no one be harmed; its second, that common things be used as common. Its foundation is fides: dictorum conventorumque constantia et veritas, constancy and truth in what is said and what is agreed (I.23). Injustice has two kinds: that of the one who inflicts a wrong, and that of the one who, able to repel it, does not (I.23). Omission is not the lesser kind. Cicero places the one who leaves the injured undefended in the same fault as the one who abandons parents, friends, or country.

Two further passages bear on the structure of this record. The first is the guardian's rule (I.85), which Cicero takes from Plato in two precepts. Public office is a tutela, a guardianship, administered for the benefit of those committed to it and not of those to whom it is committed; and one who governs must care for the whole body of the republic, lest in guarding one part the rest is abandoned. A function distributed across three departments, each tending its component and none the whole, is the condition that rule anticipates. The second is the proverb Cicero reports as already worn with use (I.33): summum ius summa iniuria, the utmost of law is the utmost of injustice. Formal correctness at every stage, aggregating into a wrong that no stage commits, is its institutional form. The record in this Tairiscint documents that form across twenty years and six departments of State. The condition is structural: no single officer is required to produce it, and no change of officer has interrupted it. Four duties were owed. Four duties were not discharged. Administrative law provides the instruments through which their discharge will now be compelled. But the law does not originate the obligation — it enforces what ethical and constitutional duty already requires. The ethical breach is the primary fact. The legal proceedings are its consequence.

  • I The Duty to Reason. Every exercise of public authority over a person's rights and legitimate expectations requires a reasoned account of that exercise — not a conclusion reached in the dark, not a silence that performs the role of decision, but a determination: reasoned, attributable, reviewable. The duty to reason is not procedural decoration. It is the minimum condition under which power remains power and does not become domination. Twenty years of non-determination is twenty years of domination without account.
  • II The Duty of Care. When the State accepted custody of an OR corpus with documented applications to civil defence, emergency logistics, and national preparedness, it assumed an obligation of stewardship — not merely legal custody but the care a guardian owes to what has been entrusted to it (tutela, in Cicero's term). Storm Éowyn, Storm Darragh, COVID-19: each is a dated event in which an emergency-planning body was operating while the corpus lay undetermined. No causal claim is made. The breach is the holding without determination, and it has operational context.
  • III The Fiduciary Duty. The State acts in a fiduciary capacity when it holds information asymmetric to the public good — when it knows, or should know, what the citizen placing that information before it cannot compel it to use. Each ministerial portfolio through which this record passed held a position of institutional advantage over the person whose corpus it held. That advantage creates a duty of fidelity — fides, in Cicero's sense of constancy in what is said and agreed: to assess honestly, to act faithfully on what assessment reveals, to disclose when assessment discloses a gap. Not one of these obligations has been performed.
  • IV Fundamental Rights as the Outermost Constraint. The right to good administration under Article 41 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, the right to an effective remedy under Article 47, and the right to the peaceful enjoyment of one's possessions under Article 1 Protocol 1 ECHR are not litigation instruments deployed after the ethical breach has occurred. They are the constitutional codification of duties that precede any particular statute. Where those duties have been trespassed — where the form of compliance has been maintained while its substance has been systematically withheld — the Charter does not supplement the ethical indictment. It confirms it.

Administrative law asks: was the duty discharged according to its legal standard? Ethics asks the prior question: was the duty held to, in the spirit in which it was owed? This Tairiscint prosecutes both questions simultaneously. The legal standard will be applied in the courts and the Commission evaluation. The ethical question is placed on record here, in this register, for the attention of every institution that receives it — domestic and European, judicial and parliamentary, governmental and independent.

An acknowledgement is a dictum. An undertaking is a conventum. The foundation of justice is constancy in both. A body that issues the acknowledgement and withholds the determination has declined no duty openly. It has displayed one. The 2009 undertakings are recorded as made and are not recorded as performed.

This Tairiscint is not addressed to a single grievance. It is addressed to a structural condition. Twenty years of documented engagement with the Irish State —across six departments, multiple Oireachtas bodies, two EU Council terms, and successive ministerial configurations— has produced a record. That record now constitutes the evidentiary basis for a question that no document in this corpus has yet answered with the directness the evidence warrants: is the failure documented here a product of structural dysfunction, of misfeasance in the exercise of public office, or of logic so defective that it has collapsed into self-contradiction?

The three possibilities are not mutually exclusive. They are evaluated separately in Part IV, with reference to the specific incidents of the record —including the mutually exclusive OEP positions of 2006, 2009, and 2025; the McEntee foreclosure of February 4th, 2026; the deletion of formally received correspondence by the JCEUA; and the simultaneous five-department silence of September 2024. The evaluation is structural. The target is the mechanism, not any individual actor within it.

The intellectual framework of this record spans three disciplines. Lewis Mumford's analysis of Paleotechnic governance —institutions locked in mechanical procedures divorced from organic outcomes, measuring progress by procedural motion rather than substantive result— describes the system's form. Francis Bacon's demand for magistral order, accuracy, and clarity in the application of knowledge to public welfare describes the system's stated obligation. Carl Sagan's standard —extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence— provides the evidentiary discipline: the claims made here are not extraordinary. They are grounded in timestamped institutional acts across twenty years, documented with reference numbers, named officers, and specific dates. The evidence is the record. The record is this Signal.

The Irish-language framing is not ornamental. Mífheidhmiú oifige —misfeasance in office: the wrongful exercise of lawful authority— is the precise legal term for what the evidence, on one reading, discloses. Dealraitheachas —governance through the management of appearances— is the precise structural term for what the evidence, on another reading, discloses. Claonbheart —deliberate deviation from official duty— is the precise ethical term for what the evidence, on a third reading, discloses. The three readings are placed on the record. This Signal requires no single reading to prevail. It requires only that the question be determined.

The administrative law deployed in Parts III through V of this Tairiscint is the instrument through which the ethical obligations identified in the Foundational Frame are enforced. The law does not originate those obligations; it codifies them. Where the ethical breach is established — where the duties of reason, care, fidelity, and fundamental rights have gone undischarged — the legal remedies follow as consequence, not as primary event. This ordering is deliberate. It is also the ordering of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights: rights first, remedies second. Any institution reading this document should understand it in that sequence.

Where institutional design predictably converts knowledge into inaction, the Constitution does not ask whether the intent was malicious. It asks whether the duty was discharged. The answer, across twenty years, is that it was not.

Part I(A)
The Interregnum Nullificans: What It Is and What It Shows

This section is the alignment bridge between the Tairiscint Mheinhart and its companion document, the Interregnum Nullificans (IN, currently at version 19b, October 11th, 2026). The IN is the statement of record: a timestamped, institutionally addressed account of each dated event in the governance sequence from May 2006 to the present. From v19b it characterises nothing; argument and characterisation live here. The Tairiscint is the legal instrument: the constitutional indictment, charter framework, and remedy architecture that the record supports. Neither document is subordinate to the other. They are designed to be read in parallel, and this section is the key that makes that parallel reading possible.

The Ombudsman complaint (OMB-260331-CSD014) and all European parallel filings reference both documents. Any institution receiving one should be treated as having received the other.

What the Interregnum Nullificans Is

The IN is a statement of record, not a submission of argument. Its function is evidentiary: to establish, with timestamps, reference numbers, named officers, and specific dates, that a sequence of institutional acts occurred and that their aggregate constitutes a continuous, unresolved breach of statutory duty.

The title names the condition precisely. Interregnum —an interval between authorities during which normal governance is suspended. Nullificans —nullifying, voiding, reducing to nothing. The Interregnum Nullificans is the period —running from May 2006 to the present— during which the normal function of governance, the production of a reasoned determination on a formal submission, was suspended across every institutional authority simultaneously engaged. The suspension was not declared. It was produced, systematically and without interruption, by the mechanism Part II of this Tairiscint documents.

The IN does not allege that any individual officer acted in bad faith. It records what happened: which officer, at which institution, on which date, produced which response —and what that response's legal character is when read against the applicable framework. The legal characterisation is in the Tairiscint. The factual record is in the IN. The two documents are the same case stated in two registers.

Earlier versions of the IN, to v16, placed the operative condition on record in its most compressed form. That summary now stands as the Tairiscint's own characterisation: the breach of fiduciary duty is widespread; the ethical breach is clear; the security breaches are manifold; the duty to reason is repeatedly neglected; the impasse is manifest, active, and compounding; the duty of care is undischarged. It has been handed from minister to minister, from government to government, without a single statutory determination being issued —while material directly relevant to national security has been ignored at every level of the executive and legislature simultaneously.

The Three-Phase Authority Vacancy

The central structural finding of the record is that the governance failure did not occur within a single institutional configuration. It occurred across three successive phases of the same allocation of dual-use functions, each producing the same output: no determination.

A
The Consultation Phase (2000–2008)

Before the Control of Exports Act 2008, Ireland operated a dual structure: DETE held operational licensing authority, while the DFA was mandatory consultee in the licensing chain on military and sensitive dual-use applications, as described in the Forfás Report of 2004 and the Dáil record of January 19th, 2022. The first OR submission arrived in May 2006, within this structure. The record identifies no consultation outcome and no referral arising from it. The governance architecture existed; the record shows no determination issuing from it.

B
The Allocation Phase (2008–2024)

Under the Control of Exports Act 2008 and EU Regulation 428/2009 —subsequently recast as Regulation 2021/821— DETE held the licensing function and the National Competent Authority role throughout. The DFA held a consultation and advisory function related to the competent authority, together with Ireland's regime memberships, and its officers and Minister gave the 2009 undertakings. The Department of Defence held the security assessment architecture and held no competent authority designation at any material time. Across these sixteen years the record identifies no reasoned determination on the status of the material by any of the three. The absence persisted through five governments and six Taoisigh without change of output.

C
The Restatement Phase (Aug. 22nd, 2024–present)

The Control of Exports Act 2023 commenced on August 22nd, 2024 and restated DETE's statutory basis. It did not move a designation between departments. The record identifies no audit of unresolved matters on commencement, no disclosure of earlier submissions to DETE, and no mechanism by which DETE would assess material held or received elsewhere. That is stated as the absence of an identified mechanism, not a proven omission. DETE holds the competent authority function, and a request filed with it on September 10th, 2026 (DETE-MBURK-02755-2026) is before it. The absence of determination continues under a restated statutory basis.

The three phases are not discrete failures. They are successive expressions of a single continuous absence. A restatement of statutory basis does not discharge undertakings given under an earlier allocation of functions. What is invariant across all three phases is the absence of a reasoned determination on the status of the material. That invariance is the Interregnum Nullificans.

The Present Architecture and Its Compliance Consequence

The 2023 Act did not produce a unified competent authority, and it moved no designation. The functions a dual-use determination needs are held across three institutions: DETE (licensing and the National Competent Authority role), DFA (consultation and advisory function; Wassenaar Arrangement, NSG, MTCR, and Australia Group regime memberships), and the Department of Defence (security assessment architecture and OEP oversight; no competent authority designation at any material time). No statutory mechanism requires any of the three to consult the others before a determination is made.

The function Regulation 2021/821 contemplates —an integrated assessment with simultaneous access to the commercial export dimension and the security application dimension— is not assembled by any statute within the current Irish structure. The Commission's January 2024 White Paper on Export Controls (COM(2024) 25) identified the absence of coordinated political oversight and the failure to integrate licensing, regime-membership, and security assessment as primary EU-wide compliance failures. Ireland's present architecture exhibits all three. This is a structural compliance failure —not a procedural concern— that entered the Commission's mandatory evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 on September 10th, 2026 as an open item.

The COMPET-track choke-points through which this record enters the evaluation are identified in the forum-and-timing annex, maintained separately from the IN: the Council Working Party Chair, the Irish national delegate, COREPER II, the EP Rapporteur, and the DG TRADE/DG DEFIS desk officer responsible for the Article 26 evaluation. Primary material has been placed at each of these points. The evaluation proceeds on the Member State compliance record as filed, insulated from domestic administrative filtering.

The Ethical Dimension of the Record

The summary above names five categories of breach: fiduciary duty; the ethical breach; the security breaches; the duty to reason; the duty of care. They are not interchangeable. Each describes a distinct failure with its own character and consequences. The IN supplies the dated events they rest on and asserts none of them. This subsection supplies the characterisation, and keeps to the IN's limits: it makes no causal claim about any emergency and attributes no fault to any named individual. The target is the mechanism.

The breach of fiduciary duty runs through every phase of the Three-Phase Authority Vacancy. Each ministerial portfolio that held the record occupied a position of institutional advantage over the petitioner: it held information the petitioner could not compel it to use, in a context where that information's evaluation was legally required and personally consequential. The fiduciary's obligation — to act in the interest of the party whose information it holds — was not performed at any stage. It was performed in form: acknowledgements were issued, reference numbers were assigned, procedural courtesies were maintained. The substance — the evaluation, the determination, the reasoned account — was not produced across twenty years or six offices.

The ethical breach is the generic term for what the specific duties of office required and did not receive. In Cicero's register: the procedure displayed the form of engagement without its substance — summum ius summa iniuria in procedural form: the acknowledgement, the reference number, the smooth procedural motion, each formally correct, while the work the acknowledgement implied was never done. It is also the second kind of injustice Cicero names (De Officiis I.23): the failure to repel an injury when able. It is not incompetence in the ordinary sense, and it requires no bad faith in any officer. It is the systematic substitution of form for function, maintained across so many institutional boundaries that it constitutes, in cybernetic terms, a second-order failure: the system has optimised for the appearance of discharge rather than its reality.

The duty of care in the emergency planning context is the most operationally acute. Dr Connors's 2010 note — 'Operations Research can save lives immediately; this is a turn-key solution' — was not acted on. He was contacted again before COVID-19. The same non-response. The OEP's June 2025 adequacy letter was issued five months after Storm Éowyn overwhelmed the emergency response architecture the letter declared adequate. The ethical weight of this sequence is not reducible to its legal dimensions. No causal claim is made that the corpus would have altered any emergency. The claim is narrower and does not depend on causation: material identified to the emergency-planning body as relevant was held undetermined, and the body later issued an adequacy conclusion without addressing it. That is the duty of care undischarged in its plainest form.

The duty to reason is the condition under which all other duties become justiciable. Without reasoned determination, the fiduciary breach cannot be characterised as knowing; the ethical breach cannot be assigned; the duty of care cannot be assessed against a specific failure to act. The twenty-year absence of any reasoned determination is not, therefore, merely one breach among the five. It is the breach that enables all the others to remain unresolved. This is why the duty to reason is the subject of the Tairiscint's closing section, and why the Qualifying Submissions (Duty to Determine) Bill 2026 addresses this deficiency as its primary operative provision: not because reasons are procedurally convenient, but because without them, the ethical accountability that underlies every other obligation in this record remains permanently suspended.

Part II
The Mechanism: Structural Analysis

The Nullification Loop

The Nullification Loop is not a metaphor. It is the name for a closed administrative system of six discourse clusters whose output is structurally invariant: acknowledgement-without-determination, regardless of the personnel operating at any given stage. The six clusters are: (1) Acknowledgement — receipt confirmed, institutional engagement performed, no decision pathway initiated; (2) Deferral without destination — the matter referred elsewhere, no body accepting final responsibility; (3) Remit displacement — simultaneous deflection by multiple institutions, each holding that the matter falls within another body's domain, collectively producing Jurisdictional Evaporation; (4) Sufficiency assertion — a claim that existing awareness or practice is adequate, without evaluation of the specific submitted corpus; (5) Record suppression or reset — the accumulated record either formally deleted (JCEUA, October 2025) or administratively treated as a first encounter (OEP, June 2025: 'thank you for raising awareness'); and (6) Abandonment without referral — a matter dropped from active correspondence with no onward transmission to any body at all, distinct from deferral, which at least gestures toward a destination.

The diagnostic significance of the Loop lies in its personnel-independence. It has operated through six Taoisigh, multiple Tánaistí, five governments, and successive institutional restructurings. The output has not changed. Where individual administrative failure can be attributed to a particular officer's conduct, the Loop cannot be —and that distinction is legally material. It shifts the analysis from misfeasance to architectural liability, from individual accountability to systemic reviewability, and from the question of what any officer did to the question of what the design compelled.

The Hoop-Trick: Jurisdictional Alibi Through Structural Interlock

Article 15.1 of Bunreacht na hÉireann vests legislative power exclusively in the Oireachtas. Article 28 vests executive power in the Government and holds it collectively accountable to the Dáil. These provisions presuppose a constitutional architecture in which accountability flows vertically from act to officer to institution to legislature. The hoop-trick is the mechanism by which that vertical flow is broken: two offices, each formally bound by constitutional duty, are configured so that each can plausibly characterise the matter as falling within the other's jurisdiction. The Taoiseach and Tánaiste remits — revolving, switching, each carrying Article 15 and Article 28 obligations — appear interlocked until accountability pressure is applied. Under pressure, they separate cleanly. Neither office defaults. Both do. The constitutional architecture becomes the instrument of its own circumvention.

The same mechanism operates at every level of the record. The OEP held OR outside its remit in 2009; held OR within its domain in 2025 — both positions disposing of the matter without evaluation, in the same direction. DETE holds the licensing and competent authority function under EU Regulation 2021/821; the DFA holds a consultation and advisory function and the international regime memberships; the Department of Defence holds the security assessment architecture. When the Control of Exports Act 2023 commenced in August 2024, restating DETE's basis, the record identifies no audit of unresolved matters. The DOD issues a foreclosure resting on an OEP letter — a body with no function under the Regulation — and the DOD itself held no CA designation at any material time. Each institution is, locally, formally compliant. The aggregate is a complete absence of accountability. This is not an oversight. It is the mechanism of Evasocracy in constitutional form, now distributed across three departments with no coordination requirement between them.

Burke–Byrne Interference: Two Ministers, One Determination, No Coordination Mechanism

The Burke–Byrne interference is the structural expression of this problem at ministerial level. Minister Burke (Enterprise, Tourism and Employment) holds the competent authority function under Regulation 2021/821 and the Article 26 statistical reporting obligation to the Commission. Minister of State Byrne holds European Affairs and Defence across three departments simultaneously — the Department of the Taoiseach, the DFA (which retains the Wassenaar Arrangement, NSG, MTCR, and Australia Group regime memberships), and the Department of Defence (security assessment architecture and OEP oversight). Each holds part of what a dual-use determination requires. Neither holds the full institutional span. Burke's department has no access to the regime-membership framework CA assessments require. Byrne's remit covers the memberships and security architecture but not the licensing body. An integrated assessment requires a coordinated act between them. No coordination is required by statute. None has occurred. Byrne's three-department remit does not bridge the fragmentation — it personifies it.

The Autopoietic Loop: Why Domestic Remedies Are Structurally Insufficient

The theoretical framework for understanding why this condition is self-sustaining is Niklas Luhmann's account of autopoietic systems: legal and administrative systems reproduce themselves by processing communications in forms their own architecture can handle, without necessary reference to the substantive condition the input describes. Each domestic oversight body processes the submission according to its own code — jurisdiction, standing, time limits, admissibility. A technicality is sufficient. The system produces a determination that is internally valid. The substantive question remains outside every system's operational boundary. The result is a nullification loop that is self-sustaining, personnel-independent, and formally valid at every stage — and has operated through six Taoisigh and five governments without change of output. The Commission's evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 operates from outside this boundary. It receives the Member State compliance record as an environmental fact, not a communication subject to domestic procedural filtering. This is the structural property that makes the Commission evaluation a qualitatively different kind of forum than any domestic mechanism previously engaged.

Taoiseach Micheál Martin is not simply one of many silent ministers. His office issued a commitment in February 2009. On September 25th, 2009, as the petitioner's constituency TD and as Minister for Foreign Affairs, he gave a clear verbal commitment to review the material at a meeting at the petitioner's residence in Ballyphehane, Cork. Administrative Amnesia and Designed Forgetting

Administrative Amnesia is the Loop's production of institutional forgetfulness. Each new officer encounters the record as if for the first time. The OEP's June 2025 letter thanks the correspondence for 'raising awareness' on Operations Research — as if Mr Rooney had not been contacted in 2006, as if Mr Kelliher had not declined in 2009, as if all 220 Oireachtas members had not been formally notified in 2020. Where Designed Forgetting is more acute — the JCEUA's deletion of formally received correspondence in October 2025 — it does not merely reproduce ignorance but destroys the record itself. Deletion of formally lodged correspondence following declination, without transmission to a competent body, without any procedural pathway offered, is not an administrative irregularity. The OEP maintained three positions across a nineteen-year interval, each deployed as a ground for non-evaluation of the submitted corpus, none of them reconciled. The 2009 and 2025 positions are mutually exclusive on their face. If OR falls outside the OEP's remit, the 2025 adequacy conclusion is beyond its competence to issue. If OR falls within the OEP's domain and has done so since 2009, the 2009 declination was a remit error of sixteen years' standing that was never corrected, never acknowledged, and never remedied. The 2006 position is contradictory on its own terms: interest conceded, engagement opened, outcome nil. Under O'Keeffe v An Bord Pleanála [1993] 1 IR 39, a decision is irrational where no reasonable decision-maker, properly directing themselves in law, could have arrived at it. The McEntee Foreclosure satisfies this test on three independent grounds simultaneously, and on the Foreclosure-by-Proxy ground independently. Under Meadows v Minister for Justice [2010] 2 IR 701, a public body that relies as dispositive on conclusions formally challenged and never defended has not provided adequate reasons. The foreclosure satisfies this test also. Both irrationality and inadequacy of reasons are, individually, sufficient grounds for certiorari. All four grounds are present.

The Qualifying Submissions (Duty to Determine) Bill 2026

The Nullification Loop's sustained operation across twenty years has exposed an architectural gap in Irish administrative law: no enforceable mechanism currently exists requiring any public body to produce a reasoned, attributable, reviewable determination on a qualifying submission when every available domestic channel has produced only procedurally valid non-determination.

The Qualifying Submissions (Duty to Determine) Bill 2026 is the legislative architecture designed to close that gap. It is pre-published and before Seanad Éireann under reference SSCEUST-I-2026-074. Its operative provisions — the loop closure mechanism, the prohibition on sufficiency assertions, the mandatory Ombudsman backstop, the chain-of-duty principle preventing compliance clock resets on any restatement or reallocation of functions — each address a structural feature of the Nullification Loop documented in this Tairiscint. The Bill does not require any public body to accept or adopt the submitted corpus. It removes only the option of producing no determination at all.

The Bill is downstream of this record. It does not alter the remedies available under this Tairiscint. It represents the systemic correction that the record's twenty-year pattern has made necessary.

Ethics as Foundation; Law as Instrument

The legal framework set out in this Part does not originate the obligations it enforces. Those obligations arise prior to any statute, in the ethical architecture of public office: the duty to reason before exercising power over another's rights; the duty of care owed to those whose interests the State holds in custody; the fiduciary obligation incumbent on any institution that occupies a position of information asymmetry over the governed; and the fundamental-rights guarantees that the Charter of the European Union encodes as constitutional minimums. The statutes, regulations, and case authorities enumerated below are the legal instruments through which these prior ethical obligations are rendered justiciable. They are not, and should not be read as, the primary source of the obligation they enforce.

This ordering has practical consequence for European institutional readers. The Commission evaluation of Regulation 2021/821, opened September 10th, 2026, proceeds on a compliance framework. Part IV
Three Hypotheses: Structural Non-Governance, Misfeasance, or Epistemic Failure?

The record permits three structural readings. They are not presented as findings. They are presented as the range of characterisations that the evidence, on its face, supports — and that a court, a committee, or a prosecuting authority would be required to weigh.

A
Structural Non-Governance

The failure is architectural, not intentional. The system is not designed to resolve cross-departmental submissions that engage multiple statutory frameworks simultaneously. The hoop-trick operates because no mechanism exists for the constitutional hoops to remain engaged under accountability pressure. Administrative Amnesia is produced by a system without institutional memory requirements. Designed Forgetting is the emergent property of a records management architecture that treats accumulation without action as compliance. On this reading, no individual actor has acted in bad faith. The system has simply been doing what systems without feedback mechanisms do. The constitutional consequence is identical regardless of intent. The remedy is mandamus, damages, and architectural reform.

B
Misfeasance in Public Office

Misfeasance in public office requires: (1) a public officer acting in their public capacity; (2) the exercise of a power, or deliberate failure to exercise a power, in bad faith; and (3) resulting damage to the plaintiff. The record discloses the following instances that require examination against this standard: the 2009 commitment to produce a ministerial report — made in writing, by a named officer, with named witnesses — that was never produced and has never been accounted for; the simultaneous five-department silence of September 2024 following a formal joint ministerial letter naming specific EU regulatory obligations; the McEntee foreclosure of February 4th, 2026 relying on conclusions the closing officer is presumed to know were formally contested and unanswered; the JCEUA deletion of formally received correspondence in October 2025 without statutory account. Whether any of these instances meets the bad faith threshold is a question for the court. What this Remonstrance records is that each instance, on its face, requires examination against that threshold.

C
Epistemic Failure

The record also supports a third, simpler reading: not structural architecture, not bad faith, but institutional reasoning so deficient that it constitutes a category of failure in its own right. Mumford's Paleotechnic analysis applies: institutions measuring progress by procedural motion — by acknowledgements issued, by reference numbers generated, by the length of adequacy conclusions produced — while remaining inert to the meaning of any of it. A defence committee that cannot recognise Operations Research as foundational to military strategy demonstrates a knowledge gap so basic it constitutes institutional failure. An OEP that thanks a twenty-year correspondence for 'raising awareness' may simply not know what it does not know. On this reading, the breach is epistemic: the institutions were not equipped to evaluate what was placed before them, and rather than acknowledge the gap, they performed the appearance of evaluation. This is the most charitable reading. It does not diminish the legal consequences. It only changes their character — from misfeasance to negligence, from bad faith to incompetence. Both are justiciable. The remedy differs in quantum, not in kind.

Shared Conclusion Across All Three Hypotheses

On every reading, the duty was not discharged. On every reading, the harm is established. On every reading, the remedy is available. The only variable is whether the breach is architectural, deliberate, or negligent. That variable affects quantum. It does not affect liability.

Closing
On the Duty to Reason, its Ethical Foundation, and its Constitutional Enforcement

Cicero fixes the foundation of justice as fides: constancy and truth in what is said and what is agreed. The form is maintained. The substance is not. The acknowledgement is issued; the evaluation is not conducted. The undertaking is given; the report is not recorded as produced. The duty is, in every formal sense, discharged, because the procedural motion that signifies discharge has been performed. Nothing further has occurred.

This is the ethical condition the record documents across twenty years and six departments of State. It is not primarily a legal description, though the law supplies precise language for it: phainocracy, governance through the management of appearances; dealraitheachas, the substitution of form for function; acknowledgement-without-determination as the Nullification Loop's invariant output. These structural terms name what Cicero names when he sets the guardian's duty (tutela) against its formal observance, and omission against commission. The ethical indictment precedes the legal one. Administrative law is the instrument through which the ethical obligation is enforced, not its source.

The constitutional order of this State does not require good intentions of its officials. It requires reasoned action. A public officer may act in complete good faith and still violate the Constitution, if the action taken is incapable of rational justification, is arrived at without adequate reasons, or amounts to the performance of a duty rather than its discharge. A constitutional breach does not require malice. It requires only that the applicable standard — proportionality, reasons, impartiality, timely determination — has not been met. Whether it has been met is not a question of motive. It is a question of record.

Francis Bacon's demand for magistral order, Locke's insistence that political authority is legitimate only when exercised through reason in service of the governed, and Kant's categorical requirement that any principle underlying official action be capable of universal application — these are the intellectual foundation on which the modern administrative law of this jurisdiction was built. The duty to give reasons in Meadows, the irrationality standard in O'Keeffe, and the proportionality requirement in Heaney are each applications of the same proposition: public power must be exercised through reason, or it is not lawfully exercised at all. The duty to reason is not the duty to reach any particular conclusion. It is the duty to reach a conclusion — reasoned, attributable to a named authority, susceptible to review. Under Meadows, absence of reasons is independently reviewable. Under O'Keeffe, three mutually exclusive positions maintained across nineteen years and deployed serially as grounds for non-evaluation satisfy no rational decision-maker standard. Under Heaney, the proportionality test fails on all three limbs. No legitimate objective has been identified. The impairment is total. No proportionality analysis has been conducted.

The courts of this jurisdiction are the constitutional mechanism through which the duty to reason is enforced when the executive and legislature have declined to exercise it. Order 84 RSC provides mandamus, certiorari, and declaratory relief. All three are available. The record in support of each is complete. The administrative remedies have been exhausted beyond any standard previously required. The conditions for leave are satisfied. The European parallel track is activated and running concurrently. The Commission evaluation is the structural context within which the EU dimension of this record is placed for the attention of institutions outside the domestic architecture — institutions that receive the compliance record as an environmental fact rather than a communication subject to domestic procedural filtering.

The breach is first ethical. The breach is then constitutional. The breach is finally legal. The law does not create the obligation — it enforces what ethical and constitutional duty already required. That duty has not been discharged. This Tairiscint is the instrument through which its discharge will be compelled, across every forum open to it: the High Court, the Commission evaluation, PETI, and the European Ombudsman.

Bunreacht na hÉireann opens with the People acting in the exercise of their undoubted right to determine their own institutions of governance. Those institutions derive their authority from the People and are accountable to the People through the mechanisms the Constitution provides: the Oireachtas, the courts, the rule of law. Where those mechanisms have been engaged across twenty years and have produced nothing, the Constitution does not offer the executive a permanent exemption. It offers the courts a jurisdiction. The Treaties of the European Union, and the Charter of Fundamental Rights they incorporate, offer a parallel jurisdiction. This Tairiscint invokes both.
